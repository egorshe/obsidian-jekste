[On Anta Klinst and her family]
It is worth remembering that the beloved People’s Stage Artist Anta Klints was connected to Pērkone through her father’s lineage. 
In the monograph Anta Klints (1962) by Līvija Akurātere, we read that Jānis Jekste (the actress’s father) was an "incomer" from Pērkone parish. 
His father owned the single-horse farm "Meždomas" there. This grandfather—with his long coat, pockets full of treats for children,
and lively facial expressions—was very close to the actress in her childhood. From him, she inherited not only her large blue eyes but also her inclination to mimic.
Anta Klints’s own work, "It Was Not a Rainy Day" (1974), mentions Pērkone several times. She recalls the people who once walked here (especially the Maļiks family)
and specific objects like the Maļiks' house with its reed roof, the school, and the Reiņi grove. She first arrived in Pērkone before she had even started school.
Though she initially feared the rural move, she eventually came to the conclusion that right here in Pērkone was her longed-attained "Rococo land."
Here she performed her childhood labors and mischiefs: dreaming of her Rococo land, trapping a frog in a bottle, falling into a muddy ditch with a small boy,
and participating in honey-eating contests. Here she saw her first theater performance by children and attended the Pērkone summer school. Many vivid memories of her childhood years in Pērkone were carried by the artist through the years.
